# Regulatory and evidence snapshot — refresh before use

November 6, 2026 PST. This reference supports marketing review, not prescribing, dosing or legal assurances. Source IDs resolve in `../SOURCES.md`.

## Product/use decision tree
1. Identify exact finished product, active ingredient/salt, formulation/route and intended use/population. “Peptide,” “bioidentical,” “pharmacy grade,” or a supplier tab label is not a regulatory category.
2. Check FDA product approval and current prescribing information. Approval for one brand/use/route is not transferable to another.
3. Approved product used off-label: recognize the clinician's general latitude while clearly separating FDA-approved use from unapproved use; counsel/platform review of promotion remains required. [S02]
4. Compounded: no FDA premarket safety/effectiveness/quality approval. Validate the product-specific 503A/503B/state basis and prescribing justification; neither same API nor pharmacy registration makes it an approved generic. [S03]
5. Unapproved/experimental or RUO: distinguish balanced evidence-gap education, genuine governed research and a commercial patient offer. No “RUO” disclaimer masking human treatment. Block treatment promotion unless all clinical/legal/platform gates are affirmatively resolved; a blocked channel cannot be solved by euphemisms. [S04, S13, S16]

## Current high-change areas
- **TRT:** February 2025 FDA cardiovascular boxed-warning changes and BP warnings are not “no risk.” June 18, 2026 HHS/FDA **requested** removal of the age-related limitation and proposed prostate/BPH revisions. Do not claim every label already changed or repeat a 2025 restriction as definitively current for every product. Check the current prescribed product and reconcile differing guideline/label vintages with the clinical lead. Symptoms and consistently low measurements require real evaluation; optimization is not an automatic diagnosis. [S06]
- **Menopause:** FDA updated individual product labeling in 2026; the updated-product list includes Prometrium, several systemic estrogens, Estring and Bijuva. No universal claim all warnings are gone. Bioidentical can describe approved products too; ACOG prefers approved therapy over routine compounding when suitable options exist. No automatic anti-aging, weight-loss or disease-prevention claim. [S07]
- **Testosterone telehealth:** Schedule III; fourth temporary flexibility currently ends **December 31, 2026**. Audio-video, valid prescribing purpose/registrations and federal/state conditions matter; do not apply the audio-only OUD exception to TRT. Do not promise 2027 continuity without a current legal pathway and operational plan. [S08–S09]
- **Compounded GLP-1s:** FDA April 2026 update says semaglutide/tirzepatide not shortage-listed or on 503B bulks list. Essentially-copy and documented patient-specific significant-difference questions remain; adding B12 is not blanket permission. Old shortage grace periods ended in 2025. Recheck current status/court actions rather than presuming this persists forever. [S03]
- **Peptides:** October 30, 2026 Federal Register notice announces a **draft, nonbinding interim** HHS discretion policy pending 503A rulemaking; no rights established, may be revisited. Comment window stated as two weeks after publication. It is not FDA approval, not human efficacy evidence, not broad 503B permission. Mirror appendix names six substances with identity/route and ten conditions, including single-API preparations; do not import it to every combination in a catalog. Official appendix remains a verification gate. [S04]

## Clinical marketing boundaries
TRT, menopause and obesity have different indications, risk/benefit considerations, follow-up and affordability. Don't combine them into “balance hormones, lose weight and reverse aging.” No mandatory numeric hormone target, routine add-on stack or universal lab panel is dictated by marketing. Care can legitimately end in no prescription, nonhormonal options or referral. TRT fertility concerns and sexual/reproductive conditions need respectful, non-assumptive copy, not engagement bait. No dosing advice in ads or generated patient content.

## State/ownership gates
Build the actual state matrix: entity/PC ownership and MSO clinical independence; clinician license/scope/supervision; visit modality; consent; controlled registrations/PDMP; pharmacy authority/shipping; records custody and exit continuity; medical advertising/credential rules; fee-splitting/referral compensation; recurring billing and privacy. A network's “50 states” claim does not establish all product/provider/state combinations. California examples and cash-pay status do not establish national legality. [S20–S21]

## Legal / platform / house distinctions
- Truthful substantiated claims, required consents/disclosures and professional authority are legal questions under applicable laws; FDA/FTC/board guidance helps interpret but does not itself grant clearance.
- Google/Meta can reject promotion of an otherwise lawfully available service; certification is not clinical proof or unrestricted product permission.
- Default no patient-result testimonials, no medical/weight-loss before-after transformations and no patient-level ad tracking are **house launch rules** in this pack. They may be stricter than a particular law/policy; any proposed exception needs documented review, not a claim that the original rule was universally legally required.
