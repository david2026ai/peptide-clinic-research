# Source register and refresh protocol

**Snapshot/access date: November 6, 2026 PST.** Primary sources opened during the continuous offer/strategy/pack research; the key platform, DEA, compounding, HHS tracking, licensing and 2026 labeling sources were reread while drafting this pack. This register records observations, not guarantees that rules remain unchanged. Official sources outrank this text; requested/draft actions are not final labeling/regulations. Do not treat a future reader's current date as November 2026.

## Runtime refresh — mandatory house workflow
1. Determine actual date, target state/country, advertiser role, inventory and exact product/use. Open the applicable current primary sources and relevant linked subpolicies, including current Meta Business Tools/Customer Audience terms and data-source restrictions if any measurement/audiences are proposed. They are not exhaustively captured here.
2. Record actual access time/zone, page/section, effective date, policy status and a short supporting excerpt. Open current product-specific FDA prescribing information/Drugs@FDA and current shortage/compounding material. Do not rely on an old press-release risk list for a current drug label.
3. For controlled substances reopen DEA/Federal Register and state board rules; for compounding reopen FDA/HHS plus the official appendix and any superseding actions/litigation. This pack does not assume an extension after December 31, 2026.
4. Recheck targeted state ownership/licensure/telehealth/pharmacy/advertising/privacy requirements with counsel. HHS general guidance and California examples are not a national legal opinion.
5. Open official competitor pricing/product terms when quoting current comparisons. Archive relevant excerpts/quotes without patient data. Vendor claims and catalog costs remain supplier claims until independently verified.
6. If inaccessible, record URL, method, exact error and what remains unsupported. Use a sanctioned public-page fallback if available; never credential scraping, internal APIs or policy evasion. Missing current material authority blocks affected release decisions; unrelated factual drafting may continue with explicit limits.
7. Refresh again before publication, a new jurisdiction/product/platform or a material edit. House suggestion for a long-running program: weekly policy monitoring and immediate re-review on notices, safety alerts, regulatory changes or actual disapproval. Monitoring does not replace the pre-release check.

## Not exhaustive
State-specific advertising, auto-renewal/consumer protection, controlled-substance registrations, pharmacy shipping, professional ownership, fee-splitting, privacy, consent and communications rules require an actual jurisdictional matrix. FTC Act/FDA promotion applicability and required disclosures depend on the advertiser and communication. No “complete compliance” assurance is made.

## Short verification excerpts — selected high-change sources
Reopened November 6, 2026 PST immediately before archive assembly. These excerpts are limited observations, not the full conditions or a permanent safe harbor.
- **S08 DEA:** “extending the current telemedicine flexibilities through December 31, 2026.” Also describes Schedule II–V audio-video and compliance with applicable federal/state law.
- **S03 FDA:** “Tirzepatide and semaglutide do not currently appear on the 503B bulks list or on FDA’s drug shortage list.” The same update describes individual significant-difference documentation; do not omit it or extrapolate all compounding is banned.
- **S04 Federal Register:** “does not establish any rights for any person and is not binding on HHS or the public.” The notice explicitly calls the policy draft and directs readers to the HHS site for the current version.
- **S11 HHS:** “Website banners that ask users to accept or reject a website’s use of tracking technologies, such as cookies, do not constitute a valid HIPAA authorization.” Read together with the partial-vacatur notice and role/data applicability, not as a universal no-analytics rule.
- **S13 Meta:** general telehealth service promotion is separately identified; prescription promotion requires the applicable certification/onboarding/targeting path. Country and adult limitations verified in the current page.
- **S16 Google:** “Google restricts the promotion of services related to the online prescribing, dispensing, and sale of prescription drugs.” The U.S. section identifies LegitScript telemedicine accreditation and separate Google certification. Experimental-treatment and unapproved-substance restrictions are additional gates.
