# Clinical claims, evidence and commercial truth filter

Read `../../AGENTS.md`, `../../SOURCES.md`, regulatory snapshot, privacy reference and templates. This is an internal evidence/advertising audit, not clinical practice, a legal opinion or platform approval. Review factual education without suppressing it merely because it concerns medicine.

## Intake
Get the whole asset: exact text, overlays, visuals, testimonials, captions, metadata, destination, prices, product/formulation/route, jurisdiction, audience, channel and evidence. A text-only review is labeled partial. Missing product identity, governing sources or available destination blocks affected release decisions. Do not ask for patient identifiers or general-tool access to clinical records.

## Step 1 — classify every material claim
Use claim register IDs. Classify process/credential, price/access, diagnosis/eligibility, therapeutic efficacy, safety, comparative superiority, mechanism, quantitative outcome, testimonial/typicality, approval/legal status or privacy promise. Capture express **and implied** claims from visuals and net impression. “May,” “supports,” “natural” and a disclaimer do not erase an efficacy claim.

## Step 2 — identify the actual product and use
- **Approved drug, approved use:** confirm exact label, formulation/route, population and indication; present benefits with relevant limitations/risks. FDA approval is not risk-free and not an approval of the clinic.
- **Approved drug, off-label use:** distinguish it explicitly in appropriate educational/clinical context; FDA has not established safety/effectiveness for that unapproved use. Clinicians may generally prescribe medically appropriate off-label uses, subject to other law; that is not automatic permission for every advertising claim or placement. Do not mislabel it FDA-approved for the use. Escalate promotional proposals to counsel/platform review rather than declaring all discussion illegal. [S02]
- **Compounded:** not FDA-approved; pharmacy licensure, COA, same API or sourcing from an FDA-registered facility is not product approval or proof of equivalence. Require patient/product/state-specific lawful basis and medical justification; do not call an essentially-copy program lawful because it adds a vitamin. [S03]
- **Unapproved/experimental peptide:** identify molecule, salt, route and evidence. Animal/in-vitro or mechanistic evidence does not establish human benefit. HHS draft enforcement discretion is not approval, finalized listing or a universal exemption; recheck official conditions and state law. Listing in a catalog does not make it permissible to promote. [S04]
- **Research-use-only:** no human-treatment sales funnel, prescribing claims or repackaging “for research” to evade rules. Genuine research discussion is distinct from patient sales and needs its own authorized governance.
A peptide can be an approved medicine; evaluate status at product/use level, not from the category name.

## Step 3 — evidence match
Open current primary label/regulator sources first, then relevant guidelines and appropriate human research. Record publication/effective/access dates, exact section, study design, population, formulation/route, endpoint, magnitude, timeframe, comparator, adverse events and limitations. Assess the totality, not a cherry-picked abstract. Do not invent citations, clinic outcome data or numerical efficacy. A registry, anecdote, influencer, vendor deck or COA is not adequate efficacy substantiation. Quality testing is quality evidence only.

Ask whether evidence supports the **exact** advertised statement and likely impression for this audience. FTC health guidance generally expects competent and reliable scientific evidence, often randomized controlled human clinical testing for health-benefit claims; guidance is not a universal safe harbor. Counsel determines applicable FDA/FTC/state advertising obligations and the advertiser's role. Drug product-claim ads may trigger requirements beyond general service education; do not use “consult a doctor” as a substitute for required balance/risk communication. [S01, S05]

## Step 4 — vertical checks
- TRT: symptoms alone/quiz do not diagnose deficiency. No automatic qualification, universal “more T is better,” age reversal, fertility-preservation promise or routine add-on stack. Check current product labels, including the distinction between June 2026 requested changes and implemented labeling; controlled-substance telehealth is separately gated. [S06, S08–S09]
- Menopause: systemic/local and hormonal/nonhormonal options differ. “Bioidentical” is not synonymous with compounded or safer. Do not promise HRT cures all symptoms, prevents all disease or causes weight loss. Current label revisions do not erase all risks. [S07]
- Obesity: distinguish diabetes/weight-management indications, brands/routes and patient populations. No guaranteed pounds/timeframe, “all doses same price” without support or effortless fat-loss visual claim. Do not transfer an approved injectable's evidence to a sublingual compound. [S02–S03]
- Experimental peptides: no injury-healing, anti-aging, regeneration or performance benefit claims without adequate matched human evidence and a lawful/platform-eligible basis. Preserve balanced evidence-gap education.

## Step 5 — testimonials and outcomes
Verify consent/authorization, actual experience, exact quotation, compensation/material connections and rights. No fabricated reviews, stock/AI faces passed off as actual patients, fake clinician authority or selective manipulation of reviews. Patient experience alone cannot substantiate therapeutic effects. Specific extraordinary results can imply typicality: establish and disclose generally expected results where required; “results vary/not typical” alone is not sufficient. A qualifier cannot cure a false claim. Distinguish a truthful service-experience statement from a physiological outcome statement. [S10]

**HOUSE default:** no patient-result testimonials or medical/weight-loss before-after transformations in launch campaigns pending specific clinical/legal/platform approval. This is not a claim that every before-after image or all testimonials are universally unlawful. Later review must include authentic/comparable images, typicality, privacy and platform-specific context.

## Step 6 — commercial and privacy truth
Audit first/recurring prices, prepaid term, dose/quantity tiers, labs/meds/shipping/reviews, cancellation, prescription denial/refund, state availability and appointment timing. Require source/quote and denominator for “from” and savings comparisons. No universal HIPAA-compliant/FDA-approved clinic claims, guaranteed certification or privacy assurances inconsistent with trackers. Read privacy reference; flags can be structural, not fixable by wording.

## Output
Use audit-report template: exact excerpt(s), visual/timecode, claim ID, taxonomy/source/date, specific reason, internal PASS/REVISE/BLOCKED, usable rewrite and evidence needed. A softer rewrite must not retain the same unsupported implication. Give a final assembled-draft recheck and separately list clinical/legal/privacy/platform/client/publication statuses. Never “APPROVED,” “legal,” “will pass Meta,” or “would be approved after changes.”

Example: “FDA-approved BPC-157 heals tendons” → **BLOCKED** for false approval/unsupported result; proposed educational replacement: “BPC-157 is not an FDA-approved treatment; human safety and benefit evidence remains limited.” This does not authorize a sales CTA or paid promotion. [S04]
