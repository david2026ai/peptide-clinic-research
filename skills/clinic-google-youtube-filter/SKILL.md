# Google Ads / YouTube clinic marketing filter

Read `../../AGENTS.md`, clinical filter, source register and privacy reference. Keep platform review distinct from medical/legal review. Neither a text PASS nor prior ad delivery predicts approval. Do not inherit legal-industry API settings, CPA thresholds, location bid modifiers or tracking scripts.

## Step 1 — identify inventory and complete asset set
Classify Search, Display, Demand Gen, paid YouTube, organic YouTube or multiple. Capture text/keywords, images/video/storyboard, overlays/captions, title/thumbnail, URLs/redirects, landing pages/catalog, country/state coverage, advertiser/domain certifications, audience types and data/measurement plan. Label partial reviews and unknown configuration. No ad-account access is assumed or expanded.

Paid YouTube follows Google Ads policy. Organic upload follows YouTube Community Guidelines; Partner Program advertiser-friendly monetization is a **separate** question. Passing one does not establish the others. Source S17 is monetization guidance, not a promise of recommendations, paid approval or community-guidelines compliance.

## Step 2 — healthcare/product pathway [PLATFORM S16]
Reopen the relevant country-specific policy. For U.S. online prescription telemedicine, verify applicable LegitScript healthcare certification and Google certification for the actual domain/advertiser/service. Pharmacies have distinct accreditation routes; do not interchange them. Check current restricted drug-term rules for keywords, ad text and destination, and the advertiser role. Do not blanket-ban all condition names or all legitimate healthcare Search terms.

Promotion of speculative/experimental treatments and prohibited unapproved substances can be disallowed despite a legal-access claim. An off-label clinical option is not identical to an unapproved drug, but does not give unrestricted promotional permission. General educational wording is not a loophole if the full destination sells a prohibited service. Scope any narrow educational exception from the actual policy; do not extend an exception for a different treatment category to every peptide. No cloak, domain hopping, drug-name obfuscation or different account to evade review.

## Step 3 — claims, business identity and access
Check misrepresentation: real clinical entity/credentials, actual states and visit availability, transparent charges/renewal/prepayment, medication/lab exclusions and no guaranteed clinical result/prescription. Treatment discussion must match exact labels/evidence and clinician-approved materials. Do not conceal risks or conditions in tiny text. Review product-claim disclosures with counsel and the clinical lead; an ad about a medicine is not automatically a manufacturer ad, and a service ad is not automatically exempt from all requirements.

## Step 4 — sensitive health personalization [PLATFORM S18]
Health includes chronic conditions, sexual/fertility care, intimate-body functions and invasive procedures. Google's policy restricts **advertiser-curated audiences** for these services (Customer Match, your data/remarketing segments, lookalikes and audience expansion). Certain predefined/contextual approaches can be available subject to policy; custom-segment handling differs by inventory. Review exact settings, not a blanket claim that all health advertising or all audiences are prohibited.

Neutral service/educational text is different from assuming the viewer's condition or implying private health knowledge. Avoid copy such as “We know about your infertility.” Don't assume changing “you” to a third-person phrase cures a sensitive targeting violation. Search intent does not authorize uploading health lists, and a patient exclusion list is still a disclosure. State targeting must match actual authority; location filters don't replace clinician patient-location verification.

## Step 5 — measurement [PLATFORM S19; REG/GUIDANCE S11–S12; HOUSE]
Google customer-data policy prohibits sensitive health/medical conversions for enhanced conversions or store-sales uploads. Do not upload a prescription or medical-care purchase with hashed email/phone. A bland event name does not remove sensitive context. Do not generalize that policy into a claim that every aggregate metric is banned: proposed non-enhanced measurement still needs data-flow, other platform-term and privacy review.

Default house posture: no patient-level ad measurement, clinical pixel/session replay, healthcare remarketing or medical-list uploads pending an approved matrix. Aggregate reporting and synthetic tests are preferred where privacy-safe; no automatic SDK/GTM/CAPI deployment, security-header weakening or third-party attribution-tool access. Budget/optimization recommendations must use real care economics and data limitations, not presumed complete ROAS.

## Step 6 — visuals and video
Assess misleading transformations, body/sexual shame, before-after implications, sensational anatomical animations, shocking injection/gore and harmful medical misinformation. House default: no medical/weight-loss result transformations until specific review, not a universal legal ban on every comparison. Factual non-graphic education about treatment or sexual/reproductive health is reviewed by context, not automatically rejected. For organic monetization, separately apply the currently opened S17 and any required current Community Guidelines; otherwise mark that scope NOT REVIEWED.

## Step 7 — output and gate
Use the audit template: exact excerpt/keyword/frame/setting/event → category/taxonomy → current official URL/access date → issue → proposed rewrite/structural remedy → internal status. Show separate content, destination, certification, targeting, measurement, organic/paid and human-review statuses. Unknown authority/evidence/configuration is BLOCKED for release; remediable wording is REVISE. Never claim “APPROVED,” “safe to run” or “would pass after changes.” Rereview assembled variants after fixes and send only the draft package for explicit human release authorization.

Illustrative: “Menopause care appointments — view visit pricing” can describe a real service. It is not a blanket PASS if the destination offers prohibited compounds or the campaign uses a health-patient remarketing list.
