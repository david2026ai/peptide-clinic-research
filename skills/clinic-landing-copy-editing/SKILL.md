# Website copy + sequential editing + CRO

Load `../../AGENTS.md`, `../../SOURCES.md`, shared references, clinic context and reviewed claims register. Identify page purpose, ONE main action, service, audience awareness, traffic source, target states, exact offer and available substantiation. Missing context permits a clearly conditional draft, not invented proof. For video route to clinic-video-creative instead.

## Writing workflow
1. Establish a process-based value proposition with evidenced treatment information where appropriate. Write three headline/CTA options and explain the strongest; don't simply promise a cure with a footnote.
2. Draft sections: hero/subhead/CTA → verified clinical credentials and service proof → needs addressed in neutral terms → evaluation/options → how it works → price and exclusions → risks/alternatives/eligibility → follow-up → FAQ → final CTA.
3. Clarify who treats, who dispenses, state availability, in-person versus telehealth, nonemergency limitations and appropriate referral options. No implication that a marketing brand, quiz or customer-service agent makes the medical decision.
4. Separate approved drug, off-label use, compounded formulation and unapproved peptide. Label the exact product/status; a licensed pharmacy is not FDA product approval. Avoid implying identical safety/effectiveness from the same API name. Informative medical discussion is allowed; claims must match evidence and promotion restrictions.
5. Show price next to material limitations. Distinguish one-time initial consult, ongoing care, medication tiers/quantity, labs, supplies/shipping, repeat visits, renewal, upfront prepayment, cancellation and declined-patient charges/refunds. No ambiguous “$X/month” on a multi-month prepaid plan. Do not copy a supplier's wholesale “starting dose” into a consumer's all-monthly program price.
6. Deliver page copy by section, annotations tied to claim IDs, alternatives, SEO title/description and required disclosure placement. Metadata, schema, URLs and alt text are reviewable; don't hide drug claims in SEO fields.

## Seven sweeps — do in order, then loop back
1. **Clarity:** one idea per sentence/section; explain medical terms; distinguish evaluation from treatment. Preserve clinically necessary qualifications.
2. **Voice/tone:** warm, respectful, adult; no humiliation, fear, conspiracy or “you are broken” assumptions. “You” is not categorically forbidden; do not attach a sensitive condition to the viewer in an ad.
3. **So what:** connect real features to bounded benefits (a written options summary supports understanding, not guaranteed symptom resolution).
4. **Prove it:** each objective claim gets evidence. Testimonials do not substantiate efficacy. Clinical/specialist credentials, approvals, locations and support times require operational proof.
5. **Specificity:** exact factual prices, terms and visit formats; never invent odd numbers or result timelines to sound credible. Do not require quantified health outcomes for good copy.
6. **Emotional resonance:** recognize uncertainty and make care approachable. This replaces fear/agitation and exaggerated before/after emotion; no body/sexual shame or treatment pressure.
7. **Informed next step:** clearly explain costs, privacy limits, risks/alternatives and what happens after the click. Replace “zero risk”: medicine is not risk-free. A true refund term is not a safety guarantee.
After each sweep, recheck preceding sweeps; an edit must not strip evidence limitations or make disclosures unreadable. Record exact old line → reason → proposed line. Run clinical filter, platform filter for paid destinations, then client/state review; no claim of legal approval.

## CRO workflow
Research → hypothesis → prioritize → test → analyze → proposed implementation. Audit mobile readability, accessibility, actual loading/form errors, message match, price understanding, consent and care availability. Do not strip safety/clinician/privacy links to force conversion. No session replay by default; synthetic usability tests first, privacy-approved aggregate analytics if available. Patient location must be verified in care, not inferred solely from IP/ZIP.

Prioritize a test only after legal/privacy eligibility: observed problem, proposed change, patient/business metric, evidence, effort and sample-size feasibility. Predefine baseline, minimum meaningful effect, sample size/power or stated sequential design, stopping rules and adverse/complaint guardrails. Check randomization and traffic mix. Small sample = uncertainty, not a “winner.” No inherited healthcare conversion benchmark or promised lift; CTR is not a health outcome. Paid funnel success includes attended evaluations and fully costed contribution, not just form fills.
