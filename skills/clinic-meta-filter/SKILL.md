# Meta clinic marketing filter

Read `../../AGENTS.md`, clinical-claims filter, source register and privacy reference. Run after draft/evidence review and before client/state review and publication gate. This is an internal policy audit, not a simulation that guarantees Meta's actual decision. An active ad in the Ad Library is not a safe harbor.

## 1. Complete the review surface
Obtain hook/body/CTA, primary text, headline, captions, every visual/overlay, thumbnail, presenter identity, landing page and redirects, public catalog, country/state/age settings, certification/authorization proof, audience sources, forms, pixel/CAPI/server events and matching identifiers. If only a script is supplied: limit verdict to script scope and mark destination/setup/privacy PENDING. Do not access or change accounts to fill gaps without a separate authorized task.

## 2. Classify service versus prescription promotion [PLATFORM S13]
General telehealth service advertising is not identical to prescription-drug promotion. Classify from the complete ad **and destination**, not whether the first frame avoids a drug name. For the U.S. prescription-drug route, verify the applicable LegitScript status, Meta onboarding/authorization, permitted country and adult targeting; include required consult-professional/valid-prescription disclosure. The November 2026 policy names U.S./Canada/New Zealand eligible countries, not all jurisdictions. Reopen current rules on every audit; certification of a vendor/domain is not automatically certification of the advertiser and every product.

Unsafe/unapproved substances can be disallowed notwithstanding a claimed lawful compounding basis. Review each product, not “peptides” as one category. Do not disguise promotion as education with a hidden checkout, omit the destination from review, borrow a high-risk account, cloak, misspell drugs or repeatedly evade a rejected send/publication path. A policy issue that is structural stays BLOCKED.

## 3. Personal attributes and sexual/reproductive health [PLATFORM S14–S15]
Ads must not assert/imply a viewer or their family has a health condition, low hormones, infertility, sexual dysfunction or another protected attribute. “Your low T is ruining your marriage” is a flag. Neutral “TRT evaluation and follow-up” describes a service; “you” alone is not categorically prohibited. Passing reference to an audience category and a claim to know the viewer's medical state are different.

Rewrite intrusive symptom questions to service/process or general education where truthful. Do not diagnose through insinuation, shame masculinity/fertility, or sexualize menopause/ED care. Sexual/reproductive-health services require health-oriented rather than sexual-enhancement framing and applicable adult targeting. Factual discussion of menopause, sexual pain or fertility is not automatically forbidden; exact content/placement and drug policy still matter.

## 4. Health, weight and visuals [PLATFORM S15; HOUSE]
Check claims, body-part pinching, statements of physical inferiority, sensational results and harmful/misleading medical assertions under current policy. No body/age/sexual shame or needle/gore shock hook. **HOUSE:** default no medical/weight-loss before-after/result transformations or patient-result testimonials pending specific review; do not misquote this as a universal Meta ban on all before-after cosmetic content. Educational anatomy and ordinary clinician imagery are reviewed in context, not automatically rejected. Validate licenses/releases and no fake patient or specialist.

## 5. Price, access and evidence
Run clinical filter's evidence and price audit. “Starting $X” must state the qualifying plan/commitment and material meds/labs/shipping exclusions. No guaranteed prescription, 50-state access without verified service coverage, fabricated countdown or safety guarantee. A disclaimer does not cure false claims. Truthful medicine discussion can remain if the product/channel pathway supports it.

## 6. Audience and measurement [REG/GUIDANCE S11–S12; HOUSE]
Do not copy a retail/legal lead stack. Default no patient/clinical lists, health-derived lookalikes, condition-page retargeting, booking/portal pixels, clinical session replay, sensitive native-form questions or medical-purchase CAPI payloads. This is the house launch posture; precise restrictions also depend on current Meta tool terms, account data-source categorization and law. Obtain a privacy-approved event-by-event matrix and current official Meta Business Tools/data restrictions before any exception; never label unreviewed setup PASS. Hashing, server-side routing, generic event names and consent banners are not automatic solutions. Do not create/upload an exclusion list from patients as a “harmless” workaround. State availability controls and clinical location verification remain independent.

## 7. Required report
Use `../../templates/audit-report.md`. For every issue quote exact excerpt or identify exact frame/event/setting; cite current policy/authority date; label PLATFORM versus HOUSE versus REG/GUIDANCE; explain; propose a usable rewrite or structural remedy. Group repeated wording but list every affected location. Final internal PASS/REVISE/BLOCKED plus external approvals PENDING/DOCUMENTED/NOT REQUIRED WITH REASON. After fixes: “Re-review required; unresolved setup and external approvals remain,” never “will be approved.”

Illustrative: “Do you have low T and sexual problems?” → REVISE for viewer-health assumptions; “Clinician-led men’s hormone evaluations. See service details and costs.” Only a draft replacement; license, drug/destination, evidence, privacy and platform gates still apply.
