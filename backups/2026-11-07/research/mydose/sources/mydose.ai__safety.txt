Protecting patient information, supporting responsible care
MyDose combines security controls, documented care workflows, and access to licensed practitioners and pharmacies. These safeguards support the teams delivering care and the patients receiving it.
Contact Our Team
Security for patient information
Encryption during transmission and storage
Health information is encrypted when transmitted and stored. MyDose’s platform terms specify TLS 1.2 or higher for transmission and AES-256 encryption for stored data.
Access based on responsibilities
Role-based permissions give practitioners and support staff access appropriate to their work. Clinical teams review treatment information, while administrative staff work with the information needed for their assigned responsibilities.
Secure clinical communication
Messaging keeps patient questions and care team responses within the platform. Conversations are stored with the patient’s record, giving authorized practitioners context for follow-up.
Records that support accountability
Timestamped audit trails document activity such as consent, prescriptions, appointments, and treatment changes. Teams can review the history of care and export records when needed.
Security practices behind MyDose Health
The MyDose Health security program includes:
Security testing
Regular vulnerability scanning and annual independent penetration testing.
Staff training
HIPAA privacy and security training at onboarding and annually.
Incident response
Defined responsibilities, escalation procedures, and breach notification processes.
Recovery procedures
Encrypted backups and documented disaster recovery objectives.
Account safeguards
Unique user identification, session timeouts, and account lockouts.
Protected consultations
Encrypted video visits, with prior written consent required for recording.
Clinical decisions led by licensed practitioners
Assessment before treatment
A licensed practitioner reviews the patient’s history, current medications, symptoms, and relevant test results to determine whether treatment is appropriate. Telehealth care depends on practitioner licensing and the patient’s location.
Informed consent
Patients review their proposed care and complete the required consent process. Telehealth consent explains the service, its benefits, risks, alternatives, and limitations.
Follow-up throughout care
Treatment plans organize check-ins, lab milestones, and patient updates. Practitioners use this information to review progress and decide whether to continue, adjust, or complete treatment.
Defined responsibilities
MyDose provides the technology for organizing care and communicating information. Licensed practitioners make clinical decisions. Pharmacies dispense prescribed medications. Clinics manage their teams and the delivery of their services.
Prescriptions through licensed pharmacies
Prescriptions are sent to connected, licensed U.S. pharmacies for fulfillment. The platform provides fulfillment status and shipment information so teams and patients can follow the order’s progress.
Understanding compounded medications
Some prescribed treatments involve compounded medications prepared for individual clinical needs. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and do not undergo FDA review for safety, effectiveness, or quality before marketing. Patients should discuss the proposed medication, its risks, and available alternatives with their practitioner.
